Teaching manifesto

I hand this to every cohort before I teach them anything.

v1.4  ·  Nov 2026

“I hear and I forget. I see and I remember. I do and I understand.”Confucius

Why

Timeless skills, whichever path you take.

This course will not turn you into a startup entrepreneur. It equips you with timeless skills to thrive under uncertainty and navigate the inevitable highs and lows of a life and a career, whichever path you choose. Three attributes carry the weight.

The three attributes

Questions you should be able to answer yes to.

01  /  Courage

  • Can I decide and act quickly, while the outcome is still unknown?
  • Can I stay immune to social validation, and attuned to market validation?
  • Can I be contrarian?
  • Am I capable of severe sacrifices?
  • Am I resilient enough to push forward despite setbacks?

02  /  Self-discipline

  • Can I be self-driven every day?
  • Can I prioritise and focus on what truly matters?
  • Can I commit to one industry over a long period?
  • Can I focus on outcomes rather than immediate rewards?

03  /  Resourcefulness

  • Can I achieve a lot with little or nothing?
  • Can I learn quickly?
  • Can I perform under extreme ambiguity?
  • Can I work across every domain of a venture well enough to understand it before I delegate or hire?

The only way to fail this course is by not trying.

There are no good or bad outcomes in these activities. Only attempts that lead to learning.

How

Trying, reflecting, and trying again.

Try

Learning is changing behaviour, so practice is the only route

You did not learn to ride a bike or play the piano from a lecture, a textbook or a video. Entrepreneurial skills work the same way. This course minimises lectures in favour of hands-on activities, inside the classroom and out, that will challenge you. Some will feel ambiguous to execute and uncertain in outcome. No script guarantees success. They will push you past your comfort zone, and growth waits on the far side of that fear.

Reflect

Learning solidifies when you look back at the struggle

Throughout the course I will ask you to reflect on your actions and your emotions. This feels unfamiliar if journaling is new to you. Honest reflection is what builds self-awareness, and self-awareness is what makes the rest of it stick.

Repeat

Mastery is repetition, and the course is too short for it

So there are places to keep practising deliberately once it ends: the Create4Good Hackathon, the ESSEC Ventures programme, and any asymmetric bet, side project or uncertain collective endeavour you take on.

On asymmetric bets

AI

Use it. I will assume you did.

Generative AI has changed what you can do between two classes. In ESSEC Spark APAC, participants work with an AI co-founder that collapses weeks of founder work into hours. I design for that rather than defend against it.

Which moves the scarce thing in this room. Producing the artefact is cheap now. Judging it is the course.

It coaches, scaffolds and sharpens. It never decides.

Judgment means deciding what to bet on, how much to stake before the evidence is in, and when the evidence should change your mind. In here, every one of those calls is yours.

Everyone agrees judgment is the skill that matters now. The open question is how you build it without experience. This course is a tentative answer. Bring what you made with AI, then defend the judgement behind it.
